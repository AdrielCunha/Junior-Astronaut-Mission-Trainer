# Lunar Odyssey

A browser game for kids ages 7 to 12, built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer".

**Status:** in development (hackathon weekend, November 14-15, 2026). All three chapters are playable from start to end.

## The idea

You lead a crew from a base on the Moon, across the trip to Mars, to a new base on Mars. Every day you have fewer batteries than the mission needs, so every choice has a cost: a battery spent on building the rocket is a battery that does not make oxygen or power the radiation shield.

The solar storms in the game are storms that really happened. Their dates and strength come from NASA data.

## Three chapters

| Chapter | Place | Goal | Real days used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Moon base | Build the rocket | May 5-12, 2024 |
| 2 | Spacecraft | Reach Mars | May 13-17, 2024 |
| 3 | Mars | Build a base from nothing and survive the cold nights | May 18-25, 2024 |

Each chapter is a series of days. Each day: read the morning report, send batteries to the rooms, see what happened overnight. The crew's radiation dose carries from one chapter to the next. The real trip to Mars takes about 7 months; the game shortens it to 5 days.

## Moon map gameplay updates

- The first day guides players through collecting batteries at the Battery Bank, powering Life Support, and working on the rocket.
- Extreme solar storms give the player 45 seconds to power the shield. Three batteries provide full protection; partial protection reduces the impact. The countdown continues during room tasks and pauses in menus and the rover fact card.
- A driveable rover inspired by the Apollo LRV is available on the Moon. Click it or press **E** nearby to board or exit; use **WASD**, arrow keys, or ground clicks to drive.
- During missions, boarding an empty rover installs **one carried battery**, providing power for that day. Exploration mode provides power freely. The rover stays on the Moon.
- The driver uses the selected astronaut skin. Wheel tracks and lunar dust follow the rover, and its first boarding shows a short Apollo LRV fact card in English.
- Mission progress autosaves every two seconds and when leaving the page. Returning opens the title screen with the option to continue. Saved data stays in this browser profile: chapter, day, resources, base layout, astronaut/rover positions, shield countdown and night report. Task overlays and cinematic playback are not resumed mid-animation.
- Installing a rover battery plays a short insertion animation before consuming it, then turns the battery light green. The same one-battery-per-day rule still applies.
- Rocket parts appear on the launch pad as batteries reach the workshop, with stage notifications and assembly sounds. The HUD previews the progress that will be committed at the end of the day.
- Quiet machine telemetry, radio cues and rover contact vibrations use the effects volume controls. Continuous sounds stop under pause screens, tasks, hidden tabs and scene changes; these represent suit/contact audio rather than wind or sound travelling through lunar vacuum.
- The selected `lunar_icons_v6` pack supplies the HUD, reports and notices: oxygen, energy, radiation, rocket, shield, health, tools and backpack. The backpack indicates carried batteries. PNG textures and original SVG sources live in `public/icons/lunar-v6/`; extra message icons in the same style are generated by `tools/icones/make_icons.py`.
- Optional developer resource controls are available on the development server or with `?dev`.
- On the Moon and Mars, open the bottom **DEV** menu and choose **EDIT BASE & OBJECTS** (or press **B** on the surface). **Left click** selects a building, solar panel or small surface object; move the pointer to preview its position, then **left click again** or press **E** to place it. **R** turns the selected object 90° per press, cycling through four upright views rendered around the model's vertical axis. The footprint, collision grid and terrain checks update with the orientation. Moving existing objects costs no batteries and pauses the mission, including storm countdowns. The DEV menu is available on the development server or with `?dev`.
- Hold the **left mouse button and drag** to pan the map, including while an object is selected. A movement threshold distinguishes dragging from clicking; releasing after a drag never selects or places an object. The selected preview stays at its ground position during panning. Short clicks or **E** confirm valid placements; **Esc** or right click cancels the preview. The inspector also provides Place/E, Rotate/R and Cancel/Esc buttons.
- The right-side navigation column groups four matching circular buttons: zoom in, zoom out, home and a blue edit button with a white pencil, white border and subtle highlight, with Ground below. Hovering the edit icon shows **Edit base · B**. It appears on the ground during both missions and free exploration, without requiring DEV, and hides under menus, tasks and transitions. DEV access remains available.
- Hovering an editable object shows a bright yellow highlight and its name with **Click to move!**. The selected preview has a thick outline and a **READY!** or **BLOCKED!** label. Picking allows a small margin around silhouettes and remembers the object pressed, making clicks more forgiving. Target geometry and small image masks are cached; pointer previews and panning updates are processed at most once per frame.
- Select a building or surface object and choose **Duplicate** (or **C**) to create a copy preview. The original stays in place. Rotate with **R**, choose a valid free spot, then confirm with **Place/E** or a map click. **Cancel/Esc** discards the preview; **Undo** removes a confirmed copy. Copies save with the layout and increase the map count only after placement. Building copies share the existing room/resource rules; duplication does not grant batteries or extra mission progress.
- The construction sidebar uses two navy cards, about 300 pixels wide with a twelve-pixel gap. The browser displays up to ten rows per page, with larger fonts and thumbnail tiles. The first six entries are **Fuel barrels, Beacon, Generator, Canister, Tool kit, Supply crate**, in that order; other existing objects remain accessible through pagination or scrolling. The count reflects all actual editable objects on the map. Rows have real object thumbnails, subtle hover feedback and one gold **Moving** state. The selected-object card shows a green/red shield and placement result, an orange **Place** button with an **E** badge, and **Rotate/R** and **Cancel/Esc** buttons. Invalid terrain disables Place. F still centers the preview; the header retains Undo/Help/Done.
- A green/red preview explains placement restrictions: unstable crater zones, rocks, excessive slope, occupied space, service gaps and nearby crew or rover. Invalid clicks retain the selection. **Undo** (Ctrl/Cmd+Z) reverses moves and rotations for buildings and surface objects during the current editing session; confirmed positions and orientations save automatically. Scroll or use the +/- buttons to zoom; **G** toggles the grid. **Done** closes the planner. Camera rotation remains disabled.
- A six-step English field guide explains panning, selecting from the construction page, rotating, duplicating, checking terrain and placing. **Help** or **H** reopens it. The card measures and centers its content within the UI viewport and adapts on resize.

## NASA data

The data is downloaded once and stored in the game as archived data. The game does not show it as live.

| Source | Used for |
|---|---|
| [DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) space weather database | Solar flares and coronal mass ejections that become the storm warnings |
| [RadLab](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/) radiation dosimetry database | Measured radiation dose: LRO/CRaTER in lunar orbit, Curiosity/MSL-RAD on the surface of Mars |
| [Curiosity REMS weather](https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=weather&category=msl&feedtype=json) | Real temperatures on Mars for the cold nights |
| [OSDR](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/) Open Science Data Repository | Short science facts about health and plants in space |
| [LRO LOLA polar elevation](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/lro-l-lola-3-rdr-v1/lrolol_1xxx/data/lola_gdr/polar/img/) (LDEM_80S_80M, LDEM_75S_240M) | The real ground of the base on the rim of Shackleton crater, its slopes, and the zoom-out regions |
| [NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720) (LRO LROC color + LOLA) | The whole Moon |
| [NASA SVS Deep Star Maps 2020](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851) (with ESA/Gaia/DPAC) | The star sky and constellation figures, in their real directions: the 8K Milky Way and bright-star layers, rendered toward where each view looks (the Moon view, and the solar system seen from above the planets' plane) |
| [MESSENGER global mosaic of Mercury](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/a-global-view-from-orbit) (PIA15063, NASA/JHU APL/Carnegie Institution of Washington) | Mercury's surface on its 3D model |
| [NASA JPL approximate planet positions](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html) | Where the Sun, Earth and planets are on each chapter day |
| [NASA 3D Resources, Mars](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/mars/) (Viking images processed at USGS) | The Mars globe during the trip |
| [NASA Goddard Blue Marble](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Whole_world_-_land_and_oceans_12000.jpg) (public domain) | The Earth during the trip |
| [DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) space weather database | Solar flares, coronal mass ejections, particle events and ENLIL model runs that become the morning warnings |
| [OSDR RadLab](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/) radiation dosimetry database | Measured radiation dose that sets each day's storm level: LRO/CRaTER in lunar orbit, BioSentinel in deep space, Curiosity/MSL-RAD on the surface of Mars |
| [Curiosity REMS weather](https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=weather&category=msl&feedtype=json) | Real night temperatures on Mars, which set how much heating each night needs |

Each day's storm level is the day's mean dose divided by a calm day for the same instrument: below 1.6 is calm, below 10 medium, below 100 high, otherwise extreme. In the morning the player only sees a forecast range, built by the game from the DONKI events known that day (it is not an official NASA forecast); the real level is revealed at night. Shielding for the worst case wastes batteries, shielding for the best case hurts the crew. The files in `src/dados/` list every source link; the game shows them on its "Real NASA data" screen.

Oxygen, batteries and mission progress are simplified models made for teaching. They are not measurements.

## Tech

Phaser 3, TypeScript, Vite. Runs in the browser with no account and no download.

To run it locally (Node 24; the tests run TypeScript directly in Node):

```bash
npm install
npm run dev     # play at http://localhost:5173
npm test        # game rules + engine (physics, building, map projection, planet positions)
npm test        # game balance, checked against the real May 2024 storm in all three chapters
```

How the game is built (engine, scenes, zoom, art pipeline in Blender): see [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

Controls: WASD or click to walk, Space to jump, click a module (or E) to use it, mouse wheel or
pinch to zoom; zoom out past the ground to fly to the whole Moon and on to the planets.

## Explorer 3D skin

Choose **Explorer 3D** in the astronaut suit selector to use the articulated character,
carried battery stack, alternating lunar strides and magnetic ship boots. The original
six sprite suits remain available. Selection is saved; unsupported WebGL and seated rover
poses fall back to Commander. See [the feature, assets and validation guide](docs/astronauta-procedural.md)
for details and the separate animation laboratory.
